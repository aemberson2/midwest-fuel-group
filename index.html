How it works

Three steps. Zero risk.

You don't change how you fuel. You don't have to leave your supplier. You just find out what you're really paying.

STEP 01

Send us your invoices

A few months of fuel invoices is all it takes. We line up every delivery against the published wholesale rack price for that exact day and calculate your supplier's real margin.

STEP 02

We fix the margin

If the margin is fair, we tell you and shake hands. If it's not, we negotiate it down, with your current supplier or with competing quotes. Most fleets keep the same truck showing up, at a better number.

STEP 03

We keep watching

Margins creep back up when nobody's checking. We audit every invoice, every month, against the daily benchmark, and send you a one-page statement showing exactly what you saved.

Layer two

Then we go after the card

Fixing the fuel margin is half the job. How you pay for fuel is the other half, and it leaks in ways nobody ever audits. We recapture all of it.

Pump price vs. your price

Trucks fueling at retail truck stops are usually paying the cash price when a properly set up cost-plus card would put them 20 to 45 cents a gallon under it, at the same pumps they already use.

Fees nobody reads

Out-of-network charges, transaction fees, report fees, late fee traps. Fuel card statements are built so you won't look. We read every line and get the junk removed or the program replaced.

Rebates and points you never claimed

Per-gallon rebates are negotiable and most fleets never ask. Same with the cashback and points on the cards running the rest of your business. That's your money sitting on the table, and we go get it.

One thing we do differently: if a card program pays us a referral, you'll know about it, in writing, with the math shown both ways. We only recommend what puts the most money back in your pocket.

Built by someone who reads fuel invoices for a living

Midwest Fuel Group was started by Andrew Emberson, a Twin Cities business broker who has spent years valuing and selling fleet-heavy companies: excavators, trucking firms, bus contractors, landscapers.

Recasting those financials means reading fuel invoices line by line. And the same thing kept showing up: good operators, sharp on everything else, quietly overpaying on diesel because the wholesale benchmark is invisible to them.

This company exists to make that benchmark visible. We're not a fuel supplier and we don't take a nickel from one. We work for the fleet, we put every number on the table, and we disclose exactly how we're paid, always.
